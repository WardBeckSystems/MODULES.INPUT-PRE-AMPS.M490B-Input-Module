> Hi Tony,
> I think the stuff I sent was pretty much the same stuff that was posted the last time we had a long thread on racking 490s, but will be happy to post again. BTW--any mistakes in the following info are mine. Blame them on poor typing skills and short attention span.
>
> M490B
>
> Pinouts for stand alone mic pre: 
> Power:
> Z jumpered to Y and 21the +25v pins
> X and 20 jumpered25v common pins
> W and 19 jumperedthe -25v pins
>
> MODIFIED VERSION: 
>  Output:
>  16 (hot) and T (cold) 
>  shield to K and 9 (which are jumpered). 
>  Pins F and H jumpered
>  Pins 6 and 7 jumpered
> Input: 
> 1 (hot), A (cold) 
> shield to B and 2 jumpered.
>
> UNMODIFIED VERSION: 
> Power:
> Z jumpered to Y and 21the +25v pins
> X and 20 jumpered25v common pins
> W and 19 jumperedthe -25v pins
>  Output from inserts: 
>  6 and 7 jumpered (hot) 
>  F and H jumpered (cold)
>  Shield to K and 9 jumpered 
>
> 490 LINE AMPSnon-transformer units
> P2
> Power:
> Z jumpered to Y and 21the +25v pins
> X and 20 jumpered25v common pins
> W and 19 jumperedthe -25v pins
>  Input:
>  Hot to pin 1
>  Cold to pin 2
>  Shield to A and B jumpered
> Input #2:
> Hot to pin 3
> Cold to pin 4
> Shield to C and D jumpered
>  Jumper inserts F to H and 6 to 7
> P1
> Note: This configuration works for signal checking only.
> Output:
> Pin 1 hot and jumpered to pin 5
> Pin 3 cold and jumpered to pin 7
> Shield at pin 9
>
> The Dave Thomas stuff below is from correspondence I had with Dave several years ago when I had a bunch of 490H modules I was converting to mic-pres:
>
> (The following is courtesy of Dave Thomas)
> If you use two 1K resistors to strap A to B and put a 330 ohm 
> resistor across the B inputs then the B mic input is about 18db down 
> with a condenser microphone.
>
> I checked the 1k resistors with my DVM to make sure they are within 
> 2% of each other.
>
> I also made some changes to the preamp to more closely match the M470 
> circuit.
>
> I changed R27 to a 10K and R14 to a 330 ohm this gives it a bit more 
> gain.
>
> Where I took the output from the fader connections is a bit hard to 
> solder to but it is working...however, on hindsight I should have 
> attached the wires to the underside of the circuit board. You might 
> find this easier as there is more room to get the wires and the 
> soldering iron in. The M490 works with the fader output, C15- wired to 16 (outside contact) and C14- wired to M (inside contact).
>
> Jumper the fader outputs at the point they feed 
> the Logic switch and bring them out to pins 16 & M on connector P2 as 
> those are spares.
> If the logic works then you will be able to use the Channel on as a 
> mute as pick the signal up at M1 or M2 once the summing resistors are 
> removed but there is no need for this complexity when they are used 
> as stand alone mic preamps.
>
> Plus the fader output signal will be cleaner at the fader output and 
> won't pass through the logic IC's which could become noisy or 
> intermittant.

> It's been a while. I finally got the first pair of M490B modules racked. I built them into a 2 rack space wooden enclosure (as can be seen in the pictures), which turned out to be a bit of a pain to work on. The powers upply, transformer and other stuff was built onto a metal tray attached to the back of the box.The tray slides into the box. I lined the top with a metal plate for shielding too. The whole deal was pretty awkward to assemble. But they are done and they sound great. I'm Very happy with them. I needed a bit of help sorting out some stuff with the wiring and getting them running, but they are going nicely.
>
> The power supply I built and used was a kitset supplied by Graeme Rogerson here in New Zealand. It has a larger foot print than the JLM Ac/DC, and doesn't use the pump method for supplying the +48v. It works well in supplying two modules. I used a pair of JLMaudio Go-between's for phase, pad and phantom.
>
> The switch lights don't work on either of my modules, so an LED on the front panel is the only light (also phantom lights if 48v is on) to judge if the units are off or on. Removed Z2 (the CD4016) and jumpered the socket as per the photo, so that the channel on switch is permanently on. I just use a master power switch for control of the box as a whole.
>
> I used both P-1 and P-2 with edge connectors, and wired two outputs per channel. One goes directly out from 6 and 7 on P-2, and the other goes from aux out 11 and 12 on P-1. That way I can send two feeds per channel, and one of them can be either pre or post fader (from the aux). One thing that wasn't mentioned in the notes previously was to connect the shield on P-2 ( pin 2 and pin 9) via a wire so that input and output shields are connected. It could be done at the other end by connecting the input and output shields at the xLR, but it seems to make more sense to me to do it at the edge connector, but not at both the XLR and edge connector or there would be a ground loop...so it's either or. This stuff may seem simple, but I think it's worth mentioning for inexperienced people (like me) who may be looking for info on racking these modules and wonder why they don't work properly if it's not done.
>
> I think most of the time since there is no clip light or metering on the device, I will use the pre-fader (direct out) settings, so that it lessens the likely hood of clipping and distortion, but if I want to drive the transformer hard and saturate things more by having the input gain set high and the fader backed off, it is there as an added option via the aux send setup ( set to post fader) as I have it wired.
>
> These preamps really do sound great. Affordable to rack and top class performers for sure. Sound pretty similar to my M470 as far as I can tell so far. Well worth the effort.

> Just thought I'd point out that if anyone decided to buy edge connectors off universal_supply_house on ebay to save money rather than get more the expensive genuine EDAC ones.....take note the letters and numbers are on the wrong side of the connectors. Pretty confusing and potentially a risk to make big mistakes when wiring. The edge connectors work OK, but you have to line up the numbers on the edge connectors with the letters on the modules and vice versa. Also the letters go from A-V instead of A-Z on them( they don't miss out G,I,O and Q like they should do)...so basically just ignore the letters and numbers and just count them yourself...or spend more and buy EDAC connectors.
